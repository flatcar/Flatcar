# Flatcar Contributor Ladder

Flatcar's [governance](governance.md) promises "a clear path up the contributor ladder
into leadership positions". This document describes that path.

There are three roles: Contributor, Member and Maintainer. Contributions don't have to
be code. Docs, triage, answering questions, talks, blog posts, events, design and
release work all count, as described in
[Becoming a Maintainer](governance.md#becoming-a-maintainer).

Everyone in every role follows the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and the
[contributing guide](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Contributor

Anyone who takes part in Flatcar is a Contributor: opening or triaging issues,
submitting or reviewing PRs, improving docs, answering questions on Discord, Slack or
GitHub, testing releases, speaking or writing about Flatcar, or helping run events.

There is nothing to apply for.

## Member

A Member is an established Contributor who takes part in Flatcar regularly and in good
faith. Members are the "recognised Contributors" that the
[committee charters](committees/README.md) refer to.

Technical and non-technical contributions count equally: code, reviews, docs, triage,
answering questions, testing releases, talks, blog posts, events, design and community
work.

**Requirements**

- Multiple meaningful contributions to Flatcar in the previous 12 months, with at least
  three months between their first and most recent, so it's clear they've stayed
  involved rather than made one quick burst.
- Real engagement with the community, such as discussing their work in reviews or
  issues, taking part on Discord or Slack, or joining Office Hours, Developer Syncs or
  Flatcar events.
- Contributions that are their own work. AI tools are fine, as long as they follow
  [AI-Assisted Contributions](CONTRIBUTING.md#ai-assisted-contributions): the person
  understands, can explain and stands behind everything they submit. Low-effort, bulk
  or wholesale AI-generated contributions don't count.
- Sponsorship from two Maintainers who have worked with them directly, at least one of
  whom works for a different company from the person joining. Sponsors confirm on the
  PR that they know the person's work and that it meets these requirements.
- Two-factor authentication turned on for their GitHub account.

**Privileges**

- Listed as a Member in [MEMBERS.md](MEMBERS.md).
- Eligible to volunteer for the Steering and Technical Committees, as set out in their
  charters.

**How to become a Member**

1. Open a PR adding yourself to [MEMBERS.md](MEMBERS.md), with links to a few examples
   of your contributions. A Maintainer can also nominate you by opening the PR, with
   your agreement.
2. Two Maintainers sponsor you on the PR, as above.
3. The PR stays open for at least one week so other Maintainers can raise concerns.
   After that, any Maintainer can merge it, following lazy consensus.

## Maintainer

Maintainers look after the overall quality and stewardship of the project. What is
expected of them, and how to become one, is described in
[governance.md](governance.md#maintainers). Members are natural candidates.

## Stepping down and inactivity

Members can step down at any time by opening a PR to remove themselves from
[MEMBERS.md](MEMBERS.md).

A Member with no meaningful contributions in the previous 12 months may be removed by a
Maintainer through a PR, after a friendly check-in. Members who stepped down or were
removed for inactivity can come back just by asking: they open a PR adding themselves
back to MEMBERS.md, and any Maintainer can merge it, with no new nomination.

The Maintainers may also remove a Member for a breach of the Code of Conduct or for
contributions made in bad faith, such as passing off someone else's or automated work
as their own, by a simple majority vote. Someone removed this way goes through the full
process again if they want to return.
